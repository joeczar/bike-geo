Where each frame puts you

Fit the same stem and spacer stack to every frame and the bars land somewhere different. That difference is the ride position: how upright you sit, how far the reach stretches, how much drop you get. Your fit report is the datum to measure from, not a bar every frame has to clear.

Same spacers, stem and angle on every frame, so the numbers isolate what the frame itself does. Drop is saddle top to bar top; a smaller number is a more upright bike. Nothing here is a pass or a fail.

The 30 mm overhead is calibrated, not guessed. Zero spacers still leaves the stem clamp well above the head tube: headset top cup plus half the clamp height. Take Fairlight's two builds exactly as they specced them — 110 mm stem on 15 mm of spacers for the 56R, 100 mm on none for the 58R — and ask what overhead reproduces the bar coordinates their own report says those builds achieve. The answer is 36.7 mm and 36.8 mm: the same constant twice, a tenth of a millimetre apart, and exactly what a Faran's 17 mm Hope Cup 1 plus a stem clamp accounts for. Frames with a low-stack headset cup (8 mm rather than 17) sit nearer 27 mm, so 30 is the default here. Adjust it and everything recomputes.

On the extensions

FramePad topPad reachPad drop Hands reachHands dropHands vs datum

The same clip-ons on every frame, mounted at the bar clamp: pads sitting 45 mm above the bar top, hands 200 mm out along extensions tilted 10° up. Drop is still saddle top to hands, so a negative number means the extensions sit above the saddle. Nothing here is a pass or a fail either — a bikepacking bar sitting above the saddle with the extensions bringing you back level with it is a normal, useful setup.

One simplification worth knowing: the pads are modelled straight above the bar clamp centre, where real clip-ons place them anywhere from 20 mm behind that to 20 mm ahead — and armrest position is the first thing anyone adjusts. That moves the hands more than the frame differences here do, so read these as a starting position, not a fitted one.

Where it earns its keep is a frame whose grips land high and short. On the PBJ with Sklar's own 35 mm stem the bar clamp is near your datum reach but 30 mm higher, and the bar's 28° of backsweep and −112 mm reach pull the grips about 100 mm further back again. Clip-ons put that extension back without touching the frame — which is why long front centres and aero bars turn up on the same bikes.

    ROSE Backroad CF L. No longer a stand-in: the whole row is the current-generation chart, including Rose's published 48 mm fork offset, and closing their wheelbase against it lands on 48.3 — so nothing here is derived. This replaced an earlier row that carried the previous generation's angles, seat tube, head tube and chainstay, which had the head angle half a degree steep and the offset 8 mm short, and so understated the trail. Rose have a Hamburg store.

    † Sklar PBJ. Sklar's own chart, so stack, reach, angles and the 52 mm offset are published rather than derived. Standover isn't in the chart, so it's blank rather than guessed. Two of their own numbers disagree, though: closing the published wheelbase would want 63 mm of offset on the M and 56 on the L, but closing the published front centre — 730 on the M, 755 on the L — gives 52.1 and 53.2 against their stated 52. The front centre is the number that fits, so the front wheel here is placed from it, which makes the wheelbase in the table 1153 and 1179 rather than the 1165 and 1182 Sklar print.

    The stem is the thing to hold in mind when reading its bar position. Sklar design the PBJ around a 30–50 mm stem and ship complete builds with a 35, paired with their swept-back riser bar, which pulls the hands back again. This page fits a 90 mm stem to every frame, so the PBJ's bar is drawn something like 55 mm further forward than Sklar intend, before any bar sweep. They also say outright that a drop bar on a PBJ is possible but hard to fit well, and point drop-bar riders at the SuperSomething instead. It's a rigid 29er on a 29 × 2.6 reference tyre.

    Sklar size by the same two numbers this page is built on. Your saddle height, measured on the Tutto in Oct 2026, is 700 mm (the fit report's 775 was 75 mm high). That is below the PBJ L's 730–790 window. On the SuperSomething it sits inside the M-L (690–750) and below the L (730–795). Sklar measure saddle tip to stem centre where the fit report measures saddle to bar, so treat that as close, not identical.

    ‡ Standert. Both charts are Standert's own, and both stop short of the two numbers this page needs for the front wheel: neither prints BB drop, and only the Erdgeschoss prints an offset. The Erdgeschoss fork is a Columbus Cross+ with a rake you choose at 47 or 52 mm; closing the published wheelbase against the 52 mm setting wants 75 mm of BB drop, and that same 75 reproduces 52.0 mm on the 58 and 51.5 on the 60 — consistent enough to trust, and the ordinary figure for a gravel frame. Its 425 mm chainstay is the shortest position of a sliding dropout. The Kreissäge RS prints neither, so 70 mm of drop is assumed as the road standard, which then derives 43.5–43.9 mm of offset — normal for a 73.5° race frame, but it is an assumption, not a published figure. Drawn on a 32 mm reference tyre against a 35 mm maximum, where the Erdgeschoss gets the 45 mm everything else here uses.

    § Otso Fenrir Stainless. Otso's own chart, but every number that touches the wheels is a range, because the Tuning Chip dropout moves the rear axle: 430–450 mm chainstay, 68–72 mm BB drop, 1150–1170 mm wheelbase, ±0.25° on both angles. The row here is the middle setting — 440 mm chainstay, 70 mm drop — which closes the wheelbase at 1150 against Otso's published 51 mm offset. The stack assumes the 495 mm axle-to-crown rigid fork, suspension-corrected for a 120 mm fork, so it is not like-for-like at the head tube against the rigid gravel frames. It is a 29er on 2.35" rubber with a 68° head angle, and like the PBJ its reach is drawn for a much shorter stem than the 90 mm fitted to every frame here.

    Sources. The Cinelli Tutto Plus reference row is Cinelli's own published chart, including their 45 mm fork rake and their 616 mm front centre — that front centre closes against the 45 mm rake to within a millimetre where their printed 1024 mm wheelbase would want 37 mm of rake, so the front wheel is placed from the front centre and the wheelbase shown is the derived 1034. 8bar, Canyon and Salsa's own charts; Stigmata via Santa Cruz through Bike Insights; CUBE's own chart for the Nuroad C:62, which prints no fork offset — closing its wheelbase derives 47 mm on both the L and the XL, and its effective top tube figures fall out of the stack, reach and 73° seat angle to the millimetre, so the chart is internally consistent. Salsa's Fargo stack assumes a 483 mm axle-to-crown fork, so it is a suspension-corrected figure. Fork offset is derived by closing each chart's own wheelbase, chainstay, BB drop and head angle — it reproduces the published offset within 1 mm where one exists, and supplies the Grizl figure Canyon doesn't print. Santa Cruz rider-height ranges aren't in the source data, so they're omitted rather than guessed.
