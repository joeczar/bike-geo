Dialling in the Terrel against the Tutto

The shortlist page fits one shared cockpit to thirty frames, which is what you want when you are still choosing. This one does the opposite: two frames, each with its own stem and spacer stack, so you can spec a Terrel build and see where it puts you relative to the bike you already ride. The Tutto's position is the thing to measure from — it is not a target the Terrel has to hit.

Reference · as you ride it

Cinelli Tutto Plus L

35 mm of spacers below an 80 mm stem flipped to +6° — the bike as it stands in August 2026. The 2.5 mm spacer above the stem raises nothing, so it is not counted here.

Build · what you'd order

Overhead is 40 here against the Tutto's 30: the Terrel's cables pass under the stem into the headset, and that routing cover adds about 10 mm below the clamp before any spacer goes on. It opens with 10 mm of spacers so there is something to take out later — every millimetre of that is a millimetre you can drop the bar without re-doing hoses.

Saddle height is the rider, not the frame, so it is shared. Each frame's seat angle then decides where that saddle sits fore and aft and how much post is out. Every control on this page is remembered in this browser, so a build you dialled in is still here tomorrow — Reset puts it back to the Tutto's own cockpit on both sides.

Drop is saddle top to bar top; a smaller number is a more upright bike. Saddle → bar is the straight-line distance the fit report measures. Axle ahead of hands is front axle minus bar centre — how much wheel is out in front of you, which is what changes when you size down a slack frame. Nothing here is a pass or a fail: a Terrel that sits you higher and further back than the Tutto is describing a loaded-trail posture, which is what the frame is for.

Reading

    Both frames are published data, not estimates. The Terrel rows are Propain's own chart, including their 45 mm fork offset and 72 mm BB drop, so nothing about the front wheel is derived. Their geometry is suspension-corrected for a 40 mm fork, but the rigid fork they ship is that same 425 mm length, so the published stack is what you actually get — this is a like-for-like comparison at the head tube, which is not true of every frame on the shortlist page. The Tutto row is Cinelli's own chart with their 45 mm rake and 616 mm front centre; that front centre is what places the front wheel, so the wheelbase shown is the derived 1034 rather than their printed 1024. Propain print a wheelbase rather than a front centre, so the Terrel front centres in the table are derived from it — the reverse of the Tutto row.

    The two bikes are drawn on different reference tyres — 692 mm on the Tutto, 712 on the Terrel — so their ground lines and trail figures are each computed on their own wheel. Trail moves with tyre diameter, so if you put the same rubber on both, the gap between them narrows slightly. Propain print 77 mm of trail for the M against the 78 here for the same reason.

    The Jan 2026 datum is dated, and the bike has moved since. The 489 / 663 reference came off the Tutto in January, before you flipped the stem and settled on 35 mm of spacers, and its 545 / 80 were hand measurements. Your live position is the white row, not the grey one. The datum stays on the page as a fixed marker to read drift against, and the residual between the two is measurement uncertainty rather than anything the model is getting wrong.

    A negative figure in the dial-in table is not a spacer you can fit. Read it as the amount the frame's own front end already stands above the Tutto's bar with the stem sitting as low as it goes — the Terrel is a taller-stack frame and a bikepacking front end is meant to be higher. The table is there so you can see the trade: a steeper negative angle buys height back, a longer stem spends it again, and reach moves with stem length almost independently of the angle you choose.

    Propain spec short stems across the range and their sizing puts 185 cm in the overlap between M and L, with their own advice being smaller for playful, larger for smooth. Note the head angle slackens as the frames get smaller — 70.5° on the M against 71° on the L — so sizing down here adds trail rather than removing it. Clearance is 50 mm; the frame carries 10 mm of rear micro-travel through the seatstay junction, which nothing here models.
