# Zipp Service Course SL carbon seatpost (27.2 x 400, 20 mm setback) in a Sklar SuperSomething V2 M-L

Researched 18 Oct 2026. All sources accessed 18 Oct 2026. Primary sources are Zipp/SRAM PDFs and model pages and sklarbikes.com. Retailer figures are used only where marked.

## Verdict

1. **OK as-is on paper.** About 100 mm of post shows above the collar, so roughly **300 mm sits in the frame**. That is far past Zipp's 90 mm minimum, and the bottom of the post sits ~240 mm above BB centre, nowhere near the shell.
2. **No maximum insertion is stated** by Zipp or Sklar. The only real unknown is the steel tube's lower butt and the bottle-boss bolts, which Sklar doesn't document.
3. **Test-fit before clamping.** Without the clamp, slide the bare post ~300 mm in with the bottle cages bolted on. If it slides freely, run it full length.
4. **If it binds**, cut it from the bottom, not the head. 50-100 mm off (to a 300-350 mm post) still leaves 200+ mm inserted. Otherwise no cut is needed, and you keep the adjustment range.

## Zipp Service Course SL: what Zipp/SRAM say

- **Model.** The current carbon post is **SP-SC-SL-C2**: unidirectional carbon tube, two-bolt aluminium clamp, "compatible with all saddle rail types", 0 and 20 mm offset.
  - https://www.sram.com/en/zipp/models/sp-sc-sl-c2
- **Predecessor (C1) specs.**
  - Material "Unidirectional Carbon/AL-7075 head"
  - Diameters "25.4mm, 27.2mm, 31.6mm"
  - Length "400mm", offset "0mm, 20mm"
  - Clamp Al-7075, steel hardware
  - Source: https://www.sram.com/en/service/models/sp-sc-sl-c1
- **Weight: 233 g** (SRAM model and service pages for C2). A search snippet of the English Zipp page said this is "Based On: Service Course SL 27.2 0 offset". **Unverified for the 20 mm setback version**, which is likely a few grams heavier.
  - https://www.sram.com/en/service/models/sp-sc-sl-c2
- **Manual.** The only Zipp seatpost manual SRAM links is **95-6818-001-000 Rev C, © 2017**, linked from the C1 service page. The C2 service page links no manual. Treat the figures below as current Zipp guidance for the Service Course SL; **unverified whether a newer C2-specific manual exists.**
  - https://www.sram.com/globalassets/document-hierarchy/user-manuals/zipp/stems-and-bars/95-6818-001-000-rev-c-user-manual-zipp-seatposts.pdf
- **Minimum insertion: 90 mm.** The manual says "a minimum of 90 mm for Service Course and Service Course SL ... or the minimum amount specified by the bicycle frame manufacturer, whichever is longer" (manual pp. Seatpost Shortening / Installation). Some retailers list 100 mm (Pushys, Sport Factory). That is the SL Speed figure, not the SL.
- **Maximum insertion: not stated** anywhere in the manual or on the model pages.
- **Cutting: allowed.** Per the manual:
  - Measure the final length "from the front of the seatpost", accounting for minimum insertion.
  - Tape either side of the line, then cut with a 25-tooth-per-inch (minimum) blade.
  - Sand with 400 grit and clean with isopropyl.
  - No maximum cut amount is given beyond keeping the minimum insertion. The cut is made at the bottom (tube) end, since the head is fixed.
- **Length definition.** Zipp list "400mm". The manual measures length from the front of the post. **Unverified** whether the 400 includes the clamp head or is shaft only. I assumed it runs to the top of the shaft at the clamp.
- **Seat clamp torque.** "Frame manufacturer's specifications or 6-7 N·m, whichever is lower." Sklar's figure is lower, so use Sklar's 4-6 N·m (see below).
- **Saddle clamp torque (Service Course / SL two-bolt):** 5 mm hex, tighten alternately to **7 N·m**.
- **Carbon paste: required.** "Apply a moderate amount of friction paste to the inside of the seat tube and contact surface of the seatpost. Do not apply grease." Failure "could cause the seatpost to slip".
- **Prep.** Deburr the seat tube edge, smooth it with 400 grit, and clean with isopropyl.
- **Rails.** Compatible with "saddles with metal 7 mm diameter rails and composite 7x9 mm diameter rails", for 27.2 and 31.6 seat tubes. A Selle Italia SLR Boost with round metal rails is the 7 mm case, so it is fine. **Check Selle Italia's own rail torque limit** if it is below 7 N·m (unverified).
- **No reduction shims.** Post and seat tube diameter must match. The frame is 27.2, so that is fine.
- **Bike24 listing (p1402464).** Returned 403 to automated fetch, so its specs are **not verified**. Retailers elsewhere list this post with 7 N·m rail torque and 90 mm minimum insertion.
  - e.g. https://www.performancebike.com/zipp-service-course-sl-carbon-seatpost-matte-black-00.6818.057.002/p1475974

## The calculation

Inputs:

- Saddle height 700 mm, BB centre to saddle top along the seat tube (rider's figure).
- Seat tube 538 mm, assumed BB centre to top of tube, with the collar top roughly flush.
- Post 400 mm.

Assumptions (estimates, not sourced):

- **Saddle stack ≈ 45 mm**, saddle top to rail centre, for an SLR Boost (typical road saddle, 40-50).
- **Rail centre to top of post shaft ≈ 15 mm** for a two-bolt setback head (10-20).
- **Combined ≈ 60 mm** (50-70).

Working:

- Top of shaft = 700 − 60 = **640 mm** from BB.
- Exposed above collar = 640 − 538 = **≈102 mm** (92-112).
- Inserted = 400 − 102 = **≈298 mm** (288-308).
- Bottom of post = 538 − 298 = **≈240 mm from BB centre** along the tube (230-250).

Checks:

- **Zipp minimum 90 mm:** exceeded by about 200 mm. Fine.
- **Below the top-tube junction:** yes, by a wide margin. Fine.
- **BB shell:** the shell's top edge is ~20-25 mm from BB centre (estimate; BSA shell OD ~40-42 mm). The post ends ~215 mm above it. **Fine.**
- **Each 10 mm of extra saddle stack** puts 10 mm more post into the frame. The conclusion holds across any plausible saddle.

## Where it could still bind (unverified, frame-specific)

- **Lower butt.** A double-butted steel seat tube is reamed to 27.2 only at the top. The thicker lower butt near the BB has a smaller bore that a 27.2 post may not pass. On ~540 mm steel seat tubes, the lower butt typically runs 100-150 mm up from the shell.
  - That would stop the post around 150-200 mm from BB centre, below the ~240 mm it needs to reach.
  - **Likely clear, but an estimate.**
- **Seat-tube bottle bosses.** On a steel frame, overlong cage bolts or braze-through at the bosses can protrude into the bore. Typical seat-tube bosses sit ~120-220 mm above BB centre, at or just below where the post ends.
  - **Check with cages bolted on**, and use bolts that don't reach the bore.
- **Braze or weld spatter** at the top-tube/seatstay junction rarely matters for a 27.2 post in a reamed tube, but the test-fit covers it.

## What to do

- **Test-fit.** Paste-free and unclamped, slide the post in until ~100 mm is showing, with bottle cages fitted. Smooth means use it full length: no cut is needed.
- **If it stops early**, note the depth it reaches. Cut the bottom so the post ends ~20 mm above that point, keeping at least 90 mm inserted. In practice, a 50-100 mm cut (to a 300-350 mm post) would leave 200-250 mm inserted.
- **Install:** carbon/friction paste and no grease. Seat collar at Sklar's 4-6 Nm (below Zipp's 6-7, so Sklar's wins). Saddle bolts 7 N·m alternating, unless Selle Italia specify lower.
- **A shorter post isn't needed**, since Zipp only sell this post at 400 mm. Cutting is the supported way to shorten it.
