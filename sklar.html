Dialling in the SuperSomething against the Tutto

The shortlist page fits one shared cockpit to thirty frames, which is what you want when you are still choosing. This one does the opposite: two frames, each with its own stem and spacer stack, so you can spec a SuperSomething build and see where it puts you relative to the bike you already ride. The Tutto's position is the thing to measure from — it is not a target the SuperSomething has to hit.

Reference · as you ride it

Cinelli Tutto Plus L

35 mm of spacers below an 80 mm stem flipped to +6° — the bike as it stands in August 2026. The 2.5 mm spacer above the stem raises nothing, so it is not counted here.

Build · what you'd order

Opens on the M-L — the L's 850 standover is above your inseam — with the build that matches the Tutto's reach: no spacers, 90 mm stem, −6°. Its bar still sits about 26 mm higher than the Tutto's, because the M-L's front end is taller. Your parts as they are copies the Tutto's cockpit across instead. Same 30 mm overhead as the Tutto: full external routing and an ordinary EC34 top cup on a 1-1/8" straight steerer, so there is no routing cover under the stem and spacers can come out later without touching a hose. Sklar design it around a 50–100 mm stem.

Saddle height is the rider, not the frame, so it is shared. Each frame's seat angle then decides where that saddle sits fore and aft and how much post is out. Every control on this page is remembered in this browser, so a build you dialled in is still here tomorrow — Reset puts it back to the Tutto's own cockpit on both sides.

Where the bars land

FrameBar reachBar topDrop Saddle → barAxle ahead of handsPost out

Drop is saddle top to bar top; a smaller number is a more upright bike. Saddle → bar is the straight-line distance the fit report measures. Axle ahead of hands is front axle minus bar centre — how much wheel is out in front of you, which is what changes when you size down a slack frame. Nothing here is a pass or a fail: a SuperSomething that sits you higher than the Tutto is describing a rougher-ground posture, which is what the frame is for.

Reading

    What reviewers say

    • The Radavist, Spencer Harding, Jul 2026 — V2, size L, 780 mm saddle height. The only review of the current frame, and the closest to your size. "Rides incredibly well," at the road-bike-with-big-tyres end of gravel; ran 700 × 53 tyres; the carbon fork "rode like a dream." His one con is that the stack is "a bit low" — but he normally rides an XL. He is a friend of Adam Sklar and calls it "mostly vibes-based." Review
    • Bikepacking.com, Miles Arbour, Oct 2023 — V1, 60 cm. Long front centre and 70.5° head angle, so it "tracks straight" and is stable fast, "a touch twitchy" slow. Bars "nearly level with the saddle" on a short stem. Lively, but "harsh" on fast rock and washboard against a Ritchey Outback, and "a touch flexy" with heavy panniers. Cons: price for 4130, no fork triple mounts, no dropper routing. Review
    • The Radavist, Hailey Moore, Sep 2023 — V1, 54 cm. "Remarkably smooth but not noodley," "playful without feeling sketchy." The offset and long front end help in tight corners; on slow steep climbs the front "almost imperceptibly noses around." Swapped the 90 mm stem for a 60. Review
    • Bikerumor — V1, 58 cm. Unverified, the page refused automated access: snippets say fit was good but the tester wanted a stem about 10 mm shorter, and it was "point-and-shoot" descending.
    • The pattern. Stable tracking, a little low-speed wander, smooth steel that gets busy on hard chatter. Every reviewer went to a shorter stem than stock, which is worth weighing against the 80 mm this page starts from. Nobody compares it with the PBJ or the Terrel, and there are no substantive forum threads or video reviews yet.

    Both frames are published data, not estimates. The SuperSomething rows are Sklar's own chart from the frameset page (fetched Oct 2026). Sklar print a front centre and no wheelbase, so the front wheel is placed from it — and it closes: 665 mm on the L against their 55 mm fork offset derives 54.8, the M-L 55.3, the XL 55.9. Their effective top tubes fall out of stack, reach and the 73.5° seat angle to within half a millimetre on every size, and the 412 mm axle-to-crown lands where the axis says it should once a lower cup is added. The wheelbases in the table (1054 / 1081 / 1108) are derived from that front centre; the shortlist page used to carry 1058 / 1085 and has been corrected to match. The Tutto row is Cinelli's own chart, placed the same way from its 616 mm front centre.

    The two bikes are drawn on different reference tyres — 692 mm on the Tutto, 718 on the SuperSomething, which is Sklar's 700 × 48 — so their ground lines and trail figures are each computed on their own wheel. Sklar print 69 mm of trail for the M-L, L and XL; the 68.8 here agrees. The steel fork is 412 mm axle-to-crown, an ordinary rigid gravel length with no suspension correction, so the published stack is what you actually get.

    The Jan 2026 datum is dated, and the bike has moved since. The 489 / 663 reference came off the Tutto in January, before you flipped the stem and settled on 35 mm of spacers, and its 545 / 80 were hand measurements. Your live position is the white row, not the grey one. The datum stays on the page as a fixed marker to read drift against, and the residual between the two is measurement uncertainty rather than anything the model is getting wrong.

    A negative figure in the dial-in table is not a spacer you can fit. Read it as the amount the frame's own front end already stands above the Tutto's bar with the stem sitting as low as it goes — the SuperSomething is a taller-stack frame by design. The table is there so you can see the trade: a steeper negative angle buys height back, a longer stem spends it again, and reach moves with stem length almost independently of the angle you choose.

    Sizing. Sklar size by saddle height, BB centre to saddle top: L 730–795, M-L 690–750, XL 780–845. Your saddle height, measured on the Tutto in Oct 2026, is 745 mm — inside both the L and the top of the M-L, so both are real candidates. (The fit report's 775 would have put you outside the M-L; it was a hand measurement and 30 mm high.) Head angle is 70.5° on M-L, L and XL alike, so unlike the Terrel, sizing between them changes reach and stack but not trail. Standover isn't on Sklar's current chart; the figures in the table are the ones the shortlist already carried, of unrecorded source. Frameset facts: double-butted chromoly, 142 × 12 / 100 × 12 thru-axles, UDH, flat-mount brakes, 68 mm BSA, 27.2 post, 700 × 52 clearance, up to a 44T single ring, three bottle mounts, full external routing, 2520 g frame (L) and 1100 g uncut steel fork. Stock builds use a Ritchey Venturemax flared bar.
